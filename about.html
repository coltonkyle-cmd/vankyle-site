About

Colton Kyle

Real estate developer in Shreveport, Louisiana. Creator of LIHTC Explained.

I'm a real estate developer in Shreveport, Louisiana. My day job is putting affordable housing deals together: finding the site, sizing the tax credits, layering the financing and getting the apartments built.

I came to development through finance. I spent nearly a decade in the credit union industry, most recently as CFO. Along the way I've also started and run a few small businesses. That shapes how I look at a deal: start with the numbers, then figure out what it takes to make them work.

Programs I've worked with

Most affordable housing deals stack five or six funding sources to get built. These are the ones I've used:

  • Tax credits: 9% and 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (4% deals paired with tax-exempt bonds), plus federal and state historic tax credits.
  • Public housing: RAD and Section 18 conversions, RAD/Section 18 blends, moving rental assistance from one site to another through a RAD transfer of assistance, project-based vouchers, and HUD's Choice Neighborhoods Initiative (CNI).
  • Gap financing: Federal Home Loan Bank of Dallas AHP awards, CDBG-DR disaster recovery funds, housing authority Capital Fund loans, and seller financing to acquire properties.
  • Permanent loans: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac multifamily loans, and USDA Rural Development loans.
  • State programs: tax credit applications in Texas (TDHCA), Louisiana (LHC) and Mississippi (MHC).

Why LIHTC Explained

On the side I run LIHTC Explained, a short-video series and free course on how affordable housing actually gets financed and built: tax credits, capital stacks, QAPs, RAD and the rules behind them. Most people never see how these deals come together, so I explain it in plain English. It's education only, my own views, and never about specific deals I work on.
