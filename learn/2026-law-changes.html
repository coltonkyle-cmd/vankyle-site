What changed in 2026

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.8

The tax law signed July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21) made two big changes for affordable housing, both starting in 2026:

  • 12% more 9% credits, permanently. Each state's annual credit budget went up 12%. That's why the 2026 amount was $3.416 per resident.
  • An easier bond test. 4% deals used to need tax-exempt bonds covering 50% of the building and land cost. For buildings placed in service after 2025, it's 25%, as long as at least 5% of that cost is financed with bonds issued after 2025. That stretches each state's bond cap across more projects.

What didn't make it: a 30% basis boost for rural and tribal areas was in the House version but dropped from the final law.

The catch: deals grew faster than investor money, and pricing softened. See tax credit pricing.

Key points
  • Signed July 4, 2025.
  • 9% credits up 12%, permanently.
  • 4% bond test cut from 50% to 25%.
  • Rural and tribal basis boost was not included.

What the 12% increase means in dollars

Without the change, the 2026 amount would have been $3.05 per resident. With it, it was $3.416.

Example, made-up numbers: a state with 5 million residents gets about $1.8M more annual credit authority in 2026 than it would have. At roughly $1.5M per award, that's about one more deal a year, every year.

What the 25% bond test means

To get 4% credits, a building used to need tax-exempt bonds covering at least 50% of its building and land cost. Now it's 25%, for buildings placed in service after 2025 that have at least 5% of that cost financed with bonds issued after 2025.

Example, made-up numbers: a project with $40M of building and land cost used to need $20M of bonds. Now it needs $10M. A state's bond cap can stretch across about twice as many deals of that size.

Bonds are still capped (see the bond cap), so the change matters most in states where bond cap was the bottleneck.

What didn't change

  • Credits are still claimed over 10 years.
  • The 15-year compliance period and extended use stayed the same.
  • Income limits and rent caps work the same way.
  • No new basis boost for rural or tribal areas.

Side effects to watch

Deals grew faster than investor money, which softened pricing. More 4% deals can compete for the same soft money and the same construction crews. The net effect should still be more homes, but the gap on each deal doesn't disappear.

Terms in this lesson

  • Compliance period
  • Extended use
  • Placed in service
  • Soft money
