The cap behind every 4% deal

4% deals don't use the state's credit budget, but they're still rationed.

The course / Module 2: Sizing the credit / Lesson 2.7

Last reviewed November 2026

4% deals don't use the state's credit budget, but they're still rationed. The bonds they need count against the state's private activity bond volume cap.

For 2027: each state's cap is the greater of $135 per resident or $410,520,000.

Housing isn't the only use. The same cap covers single-family mortgage revenue bonds, some industrial and student loan bonds, and other uses. The state decides how to split it, and in many states housing applications far exceed the cap.

That's why the 2026 change to a 25% bond test matters. A project that used to need bonds for half of its building-and-land basis now needs them for a quarter, so the same cap can support more deals.

Key points
  • 4% deals need volume-cap bonds.
  • 2027: $135 per resident, or $410.52M minimum.
  • Housing competes with other uses.
  • The 25% test stretches the cap further.

In practice, many deals still use more bonds than the minimum when the bonds carry a good loan. The new test applies to buildings placed in service after 2025 when bonds issued after 2025 finance at least 5% of the building-and-land basis.

How states hand it out

Every state decides by law or by an allocating agency how its cap is split. Some reserve a fixed share for multifamily housing; others take applications for all uses together. Many hold lotteries, rank applications or run rounds through the year.

Unused cap doesn't vanish right away

A state that doesn't use all its cap in a year can elect to carry it forward for certain purposes, including qualified rental housing, for up to three years.
