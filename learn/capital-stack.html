The capital stack

No affordable deal is paid for by one source.

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.6

Last reviewed November 2026

No affordable deal is paid for by one source. It's a stack, and every piece comes with strings.

  • Tax credit equity. Usually the biggest piece. No monthly payment, but strict rules for decades.
  • A first mortgage. A regular loan. Rents are capped, so the loan has to stay small enough for the property to pay it.
  • Soft loans and grants. State, city or Federal Home Loan Bank money. Cheap or forgiven, but competitive and slow.
  • Deferred developer fee. The developer leaves part of their fee in the deal and gets paid later from cash flow. It closes the last gap.
Example, made-up numbers: a $20 million deal paid for with $13M of tax credit equity (65%), a $4M first mortgage (20%), $2M of soft money (10%) and a $1M deferred developer fee (5%).

Module 4, Paying for it, takes each piece apart.

Key points
  • Every deal is layered.
  • Equity is the biggest piece.
  • The mortgage is limited by capped rents.
  • Soft money and deferred fee fill the gap.

Sources must equal uses

Every deal has two columns. Uses is what it costs: land, construction, fees, reserves. See the development budget. Sources is where the money comes from. They have to match to the dollar before closing. Whatever the main sources don't cover is the gap.

The layers, top to bottom

  1. First mortgage. Gets paid first from the property's income. Sized by what capped rents can support. See why the mortgage is small.
  2. Soft loans. Get paid only from leftover cash flow, if any, and are often forgiven at the end. See soft money.
  3. Deferred developer fee. Paid from cash flow after the loans. See the developer fee.
  4. Tax credit equity. Not a loan at all. Investors are paid back in credits and tax losses, not cash.

Try it

The capital stack calculator lets you change costs, rents, interest rates and credit prices and watch the gap grow or shrink. Flip between 9% and 4% to see why 4% deals need so much more soft money.

How gaps get closed

  • Apply for more soft money (slow and competitive).
  • Defer more of the developer fee (only as much as cash flow can realistically pay back).
  • Cut costs through value engineering.
  • Get a basis boost if the site qualifies. See the basis boost.
  • Change the unit mix or the number of units.

If none of those works, the deal doesn't happen.
