What counts in eligible basis

Since credits are sized off eligible basis, every line in the budget gets a question: is it in basis or not?

The course / Module 2: Sizing the credit / Lesson 2.3

Last reviewed November 2026

Since credits are sized off eligible basis, every line in the budget gets a question: is it in basis or not?

Usually in: construction of the residential building, much of the site work tied to it, architecture and engineering, construction-period interest and fees, permits, and the developer fee (within the state's cap). Common areas like a community room or laundry count if residents aren't charged extra to use them.

Usually out: land, permanent loan costs, reserves, marketing, syndication costs, and commercial space.

Things that shrink it: federal grants used for the building reduce eligible basis. That's why many federal soft funds are made as loans instead.

Existing buildings: for a rehab deal, the cost of buying an existing building can count as acquisition basis at the 4% rate, generally if it was last placed in service (or last substantially improved) more than 10 years before the purchase, with some exceptions. The rehab work itself has to meet a minimum: the greater of 20% of the building's basis or a per-unit amount ($9,000 per low-income unit for 2027).

Key points
  • Building costs and most soft costs are in.
  • Land, reserves and permanent loan costs are out.
  • Federal grants reduce basis.
  • Acquisition basis can earn 4% credits on existing buildings.

A budget, sorted

Budget line (made-up numbers)CostIn eligible basis?
Land$1,500,000No
Building construction and related site work$14,000,000Yes
Architecture and engineering$700,000Yes

Total$19,450,000$17,300,000 in basis

In this example, about 89% of the total cost is in eligible basis. The other $2,150,000 doesn't generate any credits, even though the deal still has to pay for it.

The gray areas

Site work is a common debate. Work that's tied to the building, like grading under it or utility connections, is usually in. Work that is really part of the land, or that would last no matter what's built, often isn't. The line is set by tax rules and case law, not by the budget's labels. That's why deals lean on tax counsel and an accountant's cost certification.

Grants versus loans

If a federal grant pays for part of the building, eligible basis drops by the grant amount. That means fewer credits. So many federal soft funds, like HOME, are usually structured as loans to the partnership, which don't reduce basis. Since 2008, below-market federal loans no longer force a building down to the 4% rate.

The check at the end

Once the building is placed in service, the owner gets a cost certification, an audit of what was actually spent and what's in basis. The state uses it to set the final credit amount and issue IRS Form 8609, which the owner needs to claim the credits.
