Three numbers, one award

Every tax credit award comes from the same formula:

The course / Module 2: Sizing the credit / Lesson 2.1

Last reviewed November 2026

Every tax credit award comes from the same formula:

  • Eligible basis: what it cost to build the apartments. Land doesn't count.
  • Applicable fraction: the share of the building that's affordable, by units or by floor space, whichever is lower. If every unit is restricted, it's 100%.
  • Credit rate: at least 9% a year for a new 9% deal, at least 4% for a 4% deal.

Eligible basis × applicable fraction = qualified basis. Qualified basis × credit rate = the annual credit, claimed for 10 years.

Example, made-up numbers: $10M of eligible basis × 100% × 9% = $900,000 of credit a year, or $9 million over 10 years. The developer sells that to an investor for cash.

In practice, the state also checks the deal's funding gap and awards only what the deal needs, which can be less than the formula. More eligible basis still means more credit, which is why what counts as basis, and where you build, matter so much.

Key points
  • Eligible basis × applicable fraction × rate.
  • Land isn't in basis.
  • Claimed every year for 10 years.
  • States can award less than the formula if the deal needs less.

The formula, one step at a time

StepWhat it meansExample, made-up numbers
1. Eligible basisDepreciable cost of the building and related costs (land excluded)$12,000,000
2. Applicable fractionLower of the unit share and the floor-space share that's affordable72 of 80 units = 90%; 63,000 of 72,000 sq ft = 87.5%. Use 87.5%
3. Qualified basisStep 1 × step 2$12,000,000 × 87.5% = $10,500,000
4. Annual creditStep 3 × credit rate$10,500,000 × 9% = $945,000 a year
5. Total creditsAnnual credit × 10 years$9,450,000

Why the “lower of” rule matters

The applicable fraction uses whichever share is smaller, units or square feet. If the market-rate apartments are bigger than the affordable ones, the floor-space share drops and so does the credit. That's why most tax credit deals make every unit affordable: 100% is the simplest way to get the full credit.

The first year is usually partial

Credits start the year the building is placed in service, or the next year if the owner chooses. In the first year, the credit is based on how many apartments are actually leased to qualified households each month. Whatever is lost while the building fills up isn't gone: it's claimed in year 11. So the total stays the same, it just takes a little longer to collect.

The state checks the math three times

The credit agency has to award no more than the deal needs to be financially feasible. It reviews the sources and uses of funds when the application comes in, when credits are allocated, and when the building is placed in service. If costs come in lower, or other funding comes in higher, the final award can shrink.
