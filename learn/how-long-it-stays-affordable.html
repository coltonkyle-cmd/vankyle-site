The 30-year promise

A tax credit deal isn't a 10-year bet.

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.3

Last reviewed November 2026

A tax credit deal isn't a 10-year bet. There are three clocks:

  • Years 1–10: the credit period. Investors claim the credits a little at a time.
  • Years 1–15: the compliance period. Incomes get checked and rents stay capped. If the property breaks the rules, the IRS can take credits back.
  • Years 16–30+: extended use. At least 15 more years of affordability, recorded against the property and enforced by the state.

That's at least 30 years, and plenty of states ask for longer. California's agreements, for example, run 55 years. Ownership often changes hands around year 15, but the rent rules stay with the property. More on that in the Year 15 lesson.

Key points
  • 10 years of credits.
  • 15-year compliance period, enforced by the IRS.
  • At least 15 more years of extended use, enforced by the state.
  • Many states require longer.

Why there are two enforcers

During the first 15 years, breaking the rules can cost the investor credits, so the IRS has teeth. After year 15, there are no credits left to take back. The affordability rules keep going because they're written into a recorded agreement on the property, often called a LURA (land use restriction agreement). The state enforces that agreement, and it binds whoever owns the property.

Why many deals last longer than 30 years

Federal law requires states to favor projects that stay affordable the longest. So many states award points for longer commitments, or require them outright. California's agreements run 55 years.

What happens at the end

When the agreement finally ends, an owner may be able to move to market rents. In practice, many properties get rehabbed with new credits and a new agreement first, which resets the clock. Losing affordable units this way is a real concern for housing advocates, often called "expiring use."

Common questions

Can the rules end early? Rarely. One path, the qualified contract, is covered in the Year 15 lesson. Many states now require owners to give it up.

Do residents have to move at year 15? No. The rent rules continue through extended use.
