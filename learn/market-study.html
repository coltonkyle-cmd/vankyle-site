The market study

Before a state can award credits, federal law requires a comprehensive market study of the housing needs of low-income people in the area.

The course / Module 3: Winning the award / Lesson 3.3

Before a state can award credits, federal law requires a comprehensive market study of the housing needs of low-income people in the area. It's done by a disinterested third party approved by the state, and the developer pays for it.

The study answers one question: will people rent these apartments at these rents? Things it looks at:

  • Capture rate: the share of qualified, income-eligible renters the project needs to fill. Lower is safer.
  • Absorption: how many months to lease up.
  • Rent advantage: how far the capped rents sit below comparable market rents.
  • Competition: other affordable properties nearby, open or in the pipeline.

States set their own thresholds, and many follow national market study standards. A weak study can sink an application before scoring even starts.

Key points
  • Required by federal law before an award.
  • Third party, paid for by the developer.
  • Capture rate, absorption and rent advantage.
  • States set the pass/fail lines.
Terms in this lesson
  • Market study
Sources
  • 26 U.S.C. § 42(m)(1)(A)(iii).
  • Metric definitions vary by state; see each state's QAP and market study guide.
