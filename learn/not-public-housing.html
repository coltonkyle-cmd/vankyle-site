It's not public housing

People mix these up all the time.

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.2

Last reviewed November 2026

People mix these up all the time. Public housing is owned and run by a local housing authority, and residents usually pay about 30% of their own income in rent.

Most LIHTC properties are privately owned and privately managed. The rent is capped by the unit, based on the area's income limits, not by each resident's paycheck. A family earning less than the limit pays the same capped rent as a family right at the limit, unless they also have a voucher.

The two programs do overlap. When an old public housing site gets rebuilt, it's often financed with tax credits, and some units may keep deeper rental assistance. That's covered in the RAD lesson.

Key points
  • Public housing: owned by a housing authority; rent based on the resident's income.
  • LIHTC: usually private owners; rent capped by the unit's income limit.
  • They can be combined, especially in public housing rebuilds.

An example of how rent works

Example, made-up numbers: two families live in identical 60% AMI apartments where the capped rent is $1,050. One earns right at the limit; the other earns half that. Both pay $1,050. If the second family has a voucher, they pay about 30% of their income and the voucher pays the rest.

That's why tax credit housing alone isn't always affordable for the lowest-income families, and why many deals pair the credits with vouchers or other rental assistance.

One detail: when a family has a voucher, the voucher's share isn't counted toward the tax credit rent cap. So the total rent on a voucher unit can sometimes be higher than the cap, as long as the family's share stays within it.

Where they overlap

Many old public housing sites are being rebuilt with tax credits through RAD and Section 18. The housing authority often stays involved as a partner or landlord, and units can keep deep rental assistance. See the RAD lesson.

Common misconceptions

"It's government housing." Usually not. Most tax credit properties are privately owned and managed, and they often look like any other new apartment complex.

"Rent is based on what I make." Not in a tax credit unit, unless you also have a voucher or other rental assistance.

  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8)
