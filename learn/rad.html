How old public housing gets rebuilt (RAD)

Public housing nationwide has a big backlog of repairs, and the old funding model doesn't let housing authorities borrow against it.

The course / Module 6: The long game / Lesson 6.4

Public housing nationwide has a big backlog of repairs, and the old funding model doesn't let housing authorities borrow against it. The Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD), created in 2012, fixes that.

RAD converts public housing units to long-term Section 8 contracts, either project-based vouchers or project-based rental assistance. With a steady contract, the property can take on debt and tax credit equity to renovate or rebuild.

Residents keep key protections, including the right to return after construction without being re-screened.

Scale: Congress capped conversions at 455,000 public housing units. As of October 2025, about 188,000 public housing units had converted, and in Maryland, Tennessee and Vermont more than half of the public housing that existed in 2012 has converted, according to HUD. Some deals blend RAD with Section 18, another tool for repositioning public housing. In May 2026, HUD began winding down Restore-Rebuild (formerly Faircloth-to-RAD), which had let authorities use RAD to build new units.

Key points
  • Converts public housing to Section 8 contracts.
  • Lets housing authorities borrow and use tax credits.
  • Residents have a right to return.
  • Capped at 455,000 units.
Terms in this lesson
  • Equity (tax credit equity)
  • Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8)
  • RAD
