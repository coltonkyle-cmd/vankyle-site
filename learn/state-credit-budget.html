Your state's 2027 credit budget

Each state's annual 9% credit budget is set by a per-person formula, adjusted for inflation every year.

The course / Module 2: Sizing the credit / Lesson 2.6

Last reviewed November 2026

Each state's annual 9% credit budget is set by a per-person formula, adjusted for inflation every year.

For 2027: the greater of $3.53 per resident or $4,082,400 for small states. That's up from $3.416 in 2026, the first year with the permanent 12% increase.

Example, made-up numbers: a state with 5 million residents gets about $17.65 million of annual credit authority. Since each award is claimed for 10 years, that's about $176.5 million of total credits handed out in one year.

On top of the per-person amount, states can re-award credits that were returned by earlier deals and may get a share of a small national pool of unused credits.

Two things to remember: the budget is in annual credits, and it's almost always oversubscribed. Most states get more requests than they can fund, often by a wide margin.

Key points
  • 2027: $3.53 per resident, or $4,082,400 minimum.
  • Budgets are in annual credits.
  • Returned credits get re-awarded.
  • Demand far exceeds supply.

What's in a state's ceiling

The per-person amount is the biggest piece, but a state's total credit ceiling for a year adds up four parts:

  • The per-person amount (or the small-state minimum).
  • Credits left unused from the year before. A state gets one extra year to use them.
  • Credits returned by earlier deals that didn't use them.
  • A share of the national pool, unused credits collected from other states and split among states that used up their own.

Who gets the minimum

For 2027, the small-state minimum of $4,082,400 beats $3.53 per person in any state with fewer than about 1,156,000 residents. Bigger states get more in total, but the smallest states get more per person.

Where the budget goes

Each state's Qualified Allocation Plan (QAP) decides how to split it, often into set-asides for nonprofits, rural areas, seniors or supportive housing, with the rest competing statewide or by region. See what QAPs reward.
