Why $1 of credit sells for about 83 cents

Developers don't get a dollar for every dollar of credit.

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.7

Developers don't get a dollar for every dollar of credit. In the second quarter of 2026, investors paid about 82.7 cents on average, down from about 85 cents a year earlier.

Why the discount:

  • Investors wait. The credits come in over 10 years. A dollar in year 10 is worth less than a dollar today.
  • Investors take risk. If the property breaks the rules during the 15-year compliance period, the IRS can take credits back.
  • Investors want a return. Paying less than $1 now is how they earn one.

Some investors pay more. Banks get Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) credit for investing in certain markets, so pricing tends to be stronger where banks want CRA credit. And when supply jumped in 2026, with 12% more 9% credits, prices slipped.

Key points
  • Pricing is cents per dollar of credit.
  • Time, risk and return explain the discount.
  • CRA demand pushes prices up in some markets.
  • About 83 cents in mid-2026.

How pricing is quoted

Pricing is stated as cents of equity per dollar of credit the investor will receive over 10 years. "83 cents" means the investor pays $0.83 now for each $1.00 of credit it will claim.

Example, made-up numbers: a deal gets $1.5M of credit a year, or $15M over 10 years. At 83 cents, that's $12.45M of equity. At 90 cents, it's $13.5M. That 7-cent difference is $1.05M, which can decide whether the deal works.

What moves the price

  • CRA demand: banks that want Community Reinvestment Act credit in a market compete for deals there.
  • Investors' tax appetite: credits only help investors with enough tax liability to use them.
  • Interest rates: higher rates make investors want a higher return, which lowers the price.
  • Supply: more credits chasing the same investors pushes prices down, as happened after 2026's 12% increase.
  • Deal risk: the sponsor's track record, the market and the structure all matter.

Who's buying

Most investors are banks and large companies. Many invest through syndicators, firms that pool investor money into funds and buy into many deals. Some large banks invest directly.

Why developers care so much

Price is one of the biggest numbers in the whole deal. Every cent of price on a $15M credit award is $150,000 of equity. Try changing the price in the capital stack calculator.
