Why your rent is lower than the cap

The rent cap on a tax credit apartment is a gross rent cap.

The course / Module 5: Living there / Lesson 5.2

The rent cap on a tax credit apartment is a gross rent cap. It includes an allowance for any utilities you pay yourself, like electricity or gas. (Phone, cable and internet don't count.)

Example, made-up numbers: if the maximum gross rent is $1,200 and the utility allowance is $150, the most you can be charged in rent is $1,050.

Where the allowance comes from depends on the building. Properties with Rural Development or HUD project-based assistance use those agencies' figures. Others can use the local housing authority's schedule, or an estimate from the utility company, the state agency, HUD's utility model or an energy consumption model.

Allowances are reviewed at least once a year. A change usually takes effect after a 90-day notice period.

Key points
  • Rent cap includes utilities you pay.
  • Allowance = cap minus what you can be charged.
  • Source depends on the building.
  • Reviewed at least yearly.
