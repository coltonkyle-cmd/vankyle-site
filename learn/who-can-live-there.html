Who can live there, and how rent is set

Every deal picks one of three minimum set-asides:

The course / Module 1: LIHTC 101 / Lesson 1.4

Last reviewed November 2026

Every deal picks one of three minimum set-asides:

  • 20/50: at least 20% of units for households earning 50% or less of area median income (AMI).
  • 40/60: at least 40% of units for households at 60% of AMI or less.
  • Income averaging: at least 40% of units, with income limits that average 60% of AMI or less and no unit above 80%. See the income averaging lesson.

In practice, most deals make nearly every unit affordable, because credits are only earned on the affordable units.

Rent is capped too. Gross rent, meaning rent plus a utility allowance, can't be more than 30% of the income limit for that unit. The limit is figured using 1.5 people per bedroom, so a two-bedroom is priced off a three-person household.

One rule surprises people: a household made up entirely of full-time students generally doesn't qualify, with some exceptions such as single parents and students who receive certain public assistance.

Example, made-up numbers: if the 60% AMI income limit for a three-person household is $48,000, the maximum gross rent for a two-bedroom at 60% is 30% × $48,000 ÷ 12 = $1,200 a month, including the utility allowance.
Key points
  • Three set-aside options: 20/50, 40/60, income averaging.
  • Gross rent capped at 30% of the unit's income limit.
  • Rent is priced off 1.5 people per bedroom.
  • All-student households generally don't qualify.

Where income limits come from

HUD publishes income limits for tax credit properties every year, by county or metro area and by household size. They're called MTSP limits (Multifamily Tax Subsidy Project). The 50% and 60% limits are the ones most deals use. Pick yours below.

How rent is figured

Rent is tied to the size of the apartment, not who actually lives in it. The rule assumes 1.5 people per bedroom (one person for a studio):

UnitHousehold size used
Studio1 person
1 bedroom1.5 people
2 bedrooms3 people
3 bedrooms4.5 people

For half-person sizes, the limit is averaged between the two household sizes. Then: maximum gross rent = 30% of that income limit, divided by 12. Try it in the rent cap calculator.

What counts as income

Generally the same definition HUD uses for Section 8: wages, Social Security, pensions, child support, and income from assets above certain amounts, for everyone in the household. The property verifies it with documents before move-in, and the household signs a certification.

The student rule, in more detail

A household where everyone is a full-time student generally can't live in a tax credit unit. Exceptions include:

  • A student receiving TANF assistance.
  • A student in certain government job training programs.
  • Single parents and their children, if none are claimed as dependents by someone else.
  • Married students filing a joint return.
  • Students who were previously in foster care.

Common questions

Is there a minimum income? Federal law doesn't set one, but many properties use a rent-to-income screen. Rules on that vary, especially for voucher holders.

What if my household gets bigger or smaller? The unit's rent doesn't change with household size. Your eligibility is based on your actual household size at move-in. See what happens if you get a raise.
