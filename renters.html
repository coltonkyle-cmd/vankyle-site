Renting a tax credit apartment

Who qualifies, how rent works, how to find these apartments, and where to go for help.

Information only. I don't own, manage or lease any apartments through this site, and I can't help with individual applications. Everything here is general information with links to official resources.

What a tax credit apartment is

Tax credit apartments (also called LIHTC or "Section 42" properties) are privately owned apartments built or renovated with a federal tax credit. In exchange, the owner agrees to keep rents capped for households under certain incomes, usually for 30 years or more. They're not public housing, and they're not the same as Section 8, though you can use a voucher at one. More on the difference →

Who qualifies

  • Income: most units are for households earning 60% or less of the area median income, and some are lower. Limits depend on your county and household size, and HUD updates them every year. How the limits work →
  • Everyone's income counts: wages, benefits, child support and some asset income for every household member.
  • Students: households where everyone is a full-time student usually don't qualify, with exceptions such as single parents and married couples filing jointly.
  • Screening: properties also run normal screening like rental history. Rules vary by property and state.

How rent is set

Rent is capped by the apartment, not by your paycheck. The cap is based on the income limit for that unit size, and it includes an allowance for utilities you pay. If you have a voucher, you generally pay about 30% of your income and the voucher covers the rest. Why rent is lower than the cap → · Rent cap calculator →

How to find one

  • HUD's LIHTC database lists tax credit properties across the country. It's a research tool, so call properties to ask about openings.
  • HUD Resource Locator: a map of HUD-related housing and offices.
  • Your state housing finance agency often has a list or search of tax credit properties. Search your state's name plus "housing finance agency."
  • 211: call or text 211 for local housing help in most areas.

Many properties have waiting lists. Applying to several and keeping your contact information current helps.

If you have, or want, a voucher

  • Tax credit properties can't turn you down just because you have a Section 8 voucher. You still have to qualify like everyone else. The voucher rule →
  • Vouchers are run by local public housing agencies. Find your local public housing agency or call HUD at (800) 955-2232.

Your rights

  • A raise doesn't get you evicted. Once you qualify, a higher income generally doesn't change your rent cap or make you move. What happens if you get a raise →
  • Rents can change once a year or so when HUD updates the limits, within the cap. When rents can go up →
  • Fair housing: it's illegal to discriminate based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, family status or disability. You can report housing discrimination to HUD or call 1-800-669-9777.
  • Problems with a property: your state housing finance agency monitors tax credit properties and usually has a compliance contact.
